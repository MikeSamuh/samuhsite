Memorial Day, 2025.

The weather could not have been more pleasant in New York City, sunny with a light breeze, as we headed to Central Park for a picnic with our dear friends, Shefali and Sumeet.

Barely a month prior, we had announced the formation of Samuh. It was a true labor of love for our founding team, born out of months spent immersed in the science of human performance and its application to the world of work. Sumeet and Shefali had been following our journey closely. In a wonderful gesture of trust, they invited us to bring our concepts and practices to their fast-growing advisory business, which aspires to redefine the field of talent with new thinking and methods. It has been gratifying to play a small part in their evolution.

Our work at Samuh is inspired by a blend of rigorous science and timeless wisdom. Much of our scientific toolkit is being built alongside our founding partners at Sapien Labs. To complement this, we draw from varied fields that view teams as living systems, including Theory U, Liberating Structures, Systemic Leadership, and design thinking. These are frameworks that allow teams to sense, adjust, and transform through intention and presence.

Of course, such ideas risk remaining, and dying as, idealistic concepts, unless they find early adopters who are bold enough to experiment with them. We are so grateful to our first five partners, and they could not be more beautifully dissimilar. They include the senior leadership team of a Fortune 10 healthcare company, a top team within a Fortune 500 global tech services giant, a fast-growing restaurant chain in Romania, a specialized advisory firm redefining the talent landscape, and our very first pro-bono client, a non-profit in Egypt that nurtures orphaned children.

While the contexts are worlds apart, their aspirations are not. These teams desire to grow into better versions of themselves.

Stepping into these diverse environments has granted us one profound insight. How we work with each other shapes our environment. Our environment profoundly affects our capacity to perform. And that capacity ultimately determines our performance. While none of us can control the externalities, we can be intentional about our practices. That is exactly what the highest-performing teams do.

Whether you are currently pushing for growth, driving efficiency, or grappling with the friction of organizational underperformance, adopting a scientific, systemic, and sustainable approach pays rich dividends.

We are still learning, iterating, and looking for fellow travelers. If these ideas spark a curiosity or resonate with the challenges you are navigating today, I would love to grab a coffee, virtual or otherwise, and explore them together.

On this rainy Memorial Day, I am raising a toast. To our early adopters, thank you for trusting us with your vision. To the similarly inspired, we hope for journeys together.
