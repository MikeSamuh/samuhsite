## Summary

Of the four factors from episode 14 that organisations can influence, healthy habits, autonomy, purpose and belonging, Rahul takes the least understood: autonomy. He defines it as three things. Flexibility over time, so that work hours do not prevent someone collecting their children or caring for a parent. Flexibility over how to accomplish the work, or professional latitude, being told what to do or how to do it but not both. And a reasonable workload, where stretch goals can motivate but unrelenting hours create chronic stress and a drop in performance. Autonomy, in his framing, is about not subsuming the individual within the organisation, so that talent can be expressed fully in service of team goals. He acknowledges organisations fear it leads to chaos, but being free is not a free-for-all: autonomy always sits within boundaries, as freedom in a society sits within its rules.

## Key points

- Autonomy is flexibility over time, latitude over method, and a reasonable workload
- Latitude means being told what to do or how to do it, but not both
- Unreasonable workloads and relentless hours produce chronic stress and lower performance
- Autonomy lets people express their talent in service of the team's goals
- It operates within broad boundaries, which is what keeps it from becoming chaos
