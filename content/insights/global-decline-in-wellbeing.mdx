## Summary

Rahul argues that mental wellbeing is more than depression or anxiety, because we perceive reality through the mind and it affects every dimension of how we function. He describes three trends from the research: overall mental wellbeing has been declining for over a decade and did not recover after the pandemic, the social self has seen the sharpest drop between older and younger generations, and family relationships and close friendships are eroding. He points to three likely causes: rising individualism, money as the measure of all progress and the stress that brings into work, and the digital revolution, which reduced in-person contact and made life more sedentary. His answer is to begin by measuring. He joined the board of Sapien Labs, whose scientists studied existing assessments before creating the MHQ, and he invites viewers to take it and share it.

## Key points

- Mental wellbeing shapes physical health, capacity and the ability to function, not just mood
- He cites young adults as three to five times worse off than older generations
- The social self and close relationships have seen the sharpest declines
- Likely causes: individualism, money as the measure of progress, and the smartphone
- Measure what you treasure: the MHQ is his suggested first step
- He describes the MHQ as taking about 15 minutes and being anonymous
