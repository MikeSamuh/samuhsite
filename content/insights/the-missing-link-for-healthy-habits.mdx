## Summary

Rahul starts with the habit loop from James Clear's Atomic Habits, cue, craving, response and reward, and his own example of finally flossing by keeping the floss next to his toothbrush. If forming habits were that simple, he asks, why do New Year resolutions start with a bang and end with a whimper? His answer is that the loop leaves out the environment: the team, home, organisation and community a person lives in. He cites team performance coach Owen Eastwood's claim that 70 percent of human behaviour is driven by environment. His proposal, borrowed from wisdom cultures, is to ritualise habits. Rituals are collective, and research he cites shows that rituals which are physical, psychological and communal can improve group performance. So instead of a resolution, make a ritual with your team, at home or at work, so that people keep each other accountable and support one another.

## Key points

- The cue, craving, response, reward loop explains how habits form but not why they fail to stick
- The missing factor is environment, which he says drives most of our behaviour
- Rituals differ from habits because they are done together
- Rituals that are physical, psychological and communal can improve group performance
- Make a ritual with your team instead of a personal resolution
