## Summary

Rahul argues that most people hold wellbeing and performance in two separate compartments. Wellbeing is the soft benefits package, mindfulness apps, yoga, gym memberships, like upgrades on a car that make it nicer to sit in but do not change how it runs. Performance is the hard numbers delivered quarter after quarter, like a car expected to run forever without fuel or servicing. He calls that a fool's errand: to sustain high performance you first need a sound mind and a strong body. Using the World Health Organization's definition, mental wellbeing is about functioning well, not just feeling well. He then draws on the Sapien Labs database to say there is a clear correlation between wellbeing and performance: lower wellbeing means lower productivity and a lower ability to show up for work at all. Ignoring wellbeing, he concludes, sabotages the organisation's own aspirations for sustained performance.

## Key points

- Wellbeing is usually treated as a perk, performance as the real work
- Mental wellbeing is about functioning well, not only feeling well
- He cites Sapien Labs data showing lower wellbeing correlates with lower productivity and lower attendance
- He cites a three to five times drop in productivity between thriving and distressed, and more than tenfold in the ability to show up
- Ignoring wellbeing undermines the performance an organisation is trying to achieve
