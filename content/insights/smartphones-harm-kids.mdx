## Summary

Rahul introduces a Sapien Labs report, in partnership with Sapien Labs, which found that every earlier year of smartphone acquisition in childhood was linked to worse mental wellbeing in adulthood, with a stronger effect for girls. Tara Thiagarajan, who led the report, says the dimension most affected is the social self, and the most negative outcomes linked to early ownership are suicidal thoughts, a sense of detachment from reality and feelings of aggression. Asked what she did as a parent of three teenagers, she describes bringing her children together to look at the data, and the family agreeing to put phones down at nine, parents included. Her point is that what matters is what replaces the phone time, including the ordinary work of being at the dinner table and reading the people around you. Rahul closes by urging parents to act together, through schools and policymakers, and to model the behaviour themselves.

## Key points

- Earlier smartphone ownership was linked to worse adult mental wellbeing, more so for girls
- The social self is the dimension most impacted
- Tara's family agreed to put phones down at nine, parents included
- What replaces the phone time matters more than simply taking the phone away
- Dinner-table conversation is where children learn to read intent and social dynamics
- Act collectively: other parents, schools, local policymakers, and your own example
