## Summary

A conversation with Tara Thiagarajan of Sapien Labs, in partnership with Sapien Labs, about the MHQ, the Mental Health Quotient. Tara makes the case that mental wellbeing is often confused with mood or happiness, and that it is more than that. She uses the World Health Organization's definition: a state in which you can realise your abilities, navigate the stresses of life and contribute productively. The MHQ is meant to be an aggregate measure of our full set of mental capacities and how they affect our ability to function. She explains that psychiatry assessments look at what can go wrong, and happiness or life satisfaction indices capture only one aspect, so there was a gap. To understand how wellbeing is changing, you first need a way to measure it.

## Key points

- Mental wellbeing is more than mood or happiness
- The MHQ follows the WHO definition: realise abilities, handle stress, contribute productively
- It aims to measure the full complement of mental capacities, not symptoms alone
- Psychiatry assessments focus on the negative side; happiness indices capture one dimension
- Measurement is step one for understanding change
