## Summary

Rahul focuses on the sixth truth from his earlier episode, that wellbeing drives performance. Conventional management drives people longer and harder on the belief that harder driving means better results. He accepts that this works for some people some of the time, but calls it a recipe for stress, illness and strife in the long run, harming the very people who create great outcomes. For performance sustained over years rather than quarters, he looks to how tribes and civilisations lasted and to modern science on the body, and arrives at four principles. Healthy habits: sleep, nutrition and exercise. Autonomy: flexibility to lead a life outside work, and latitude to be told what to do or how, but not both. Purpose: knowing the work is about something bigger and valuable to others. Belonging: being part of a group with a shared mission and physical and psychological safety.

## Key points

- Driving harder produces short-term results and long-term stress, illness and ill will
- Principle one: healthy habits, meaning adequate sleep, good nutrition and reasonable exercise
- Principle two: autonomy, both flexibility over life outside work and latitude in how work is done
- Principle three: purpose, work that matters to others and to society
- Principle four: belonging, a group with shared identity, shared mission and safety
- Together these are the foundation for organisations that outlast others
