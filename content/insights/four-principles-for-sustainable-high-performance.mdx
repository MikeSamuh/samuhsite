## From Rahul

As posted on samuh.work with this episode.

The World Health Organization defines mental health as “a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the stresses of life, realize their abilities, learn well and work well, and contribute to their community.”

Our research in Sapien Labs, drawn from over 1.3 million people who have taken the Mental Health Quotient (MHQ) diagnostic, shows three concerning findings:

1. The state of mental well-being as described by WHO, i.e. the ability to function, is declining globally 2. This crisis is profoundly affecting younger generations. Young adults (18 to 24) are 3 to 5 times worse off than the oldest working generation. They will become the largest workforce demographic within the next 5 to 7 years 3. Mental well-being directly correlates with performance. People with low MHQ have 3 to 5 times lower productivity, and 10 to 12 times lesser ability to show up for work, than people who are thriving Organizations will increasingly contend with this phenomenon as a greater proportion of people have lower mental well-being than at any time before.

So what can they do? We believe that a fundamental mindset shift is needed from viewing this as an individual phenomenon to an environmental one. Fostering a healthy environment within organizations leads to enhanced well-being, and thus better individual and collective performance. From our research, we have identified four factors that organizations can directly influence towards sustaining high performance. Watch on to learn more.

Do these resonate? Are you actively taking steps towards these?

## Summary

Rahul focuses on the sixth truth from his earlier episode, that wellbeing drives performance. Conventional management drives people longer and harder on the belief that harder driving means better results. He accepts that this works for some people some of the time, but calls it a recipe for stress, illness and strife in the long run, harming the very people who create great outcomes. For performance sustained over years rather than quarters, he looks to how tribes and civilisations lasted and to modern science on the body, and arrives at four principles. Healthy habits: sleep, nutrition and exercise. Autonomy: flexibility to lead a life outside work, and latitude to be told what to do or how, but not both. Purpose: knowing the work is about something bigger and valuable to others. Belonging: being part of a group with a shared mission and physical and psychological safety.

## Key points

- Driving harder produces short-term results and long-term stress, illness and ill will
- Principle one: healthy habits, meaning adequate sleep, good nutrition and reasonable exercise
- Principle two: autonomy, both flexibility over life outside work and latitude in how work is done
- Principle three: purpose, work that matters to others and to society
- Principle four: belonging, a group with shared identity, shared mission and safety
- Together these are the foundation for organisations that outlast others
