## Summary

Rahul recounts being asked to reimagine performance management in an organisation employing hundreds of thousands of people. The question his team asked was what creates great individual and team performance. They researched companies that had outperformed competitors for long periods and went outside the corporate world to elite sports teams and defence organisations. What they found was that performance management systems had no correlation with organisational performance. Instead they identified six universal truths: do what you are great at and love, focus on the vital few priorities, treat performance as a team sport built on complementary strengths and trust, give feedback and coaching in the moment rather than as an event, replace ratings and labels with personalised actions that help people grow, and recognise that wellbeing drives performance.

## Key points

- Performance management systems showed no correlation with organisational performance
- Truth one: apply strengths, skills and passion to the work every day
- Truth two: focus on the five or seven priorities that matter, not every KPI
- Truth three: performance is a team sport, built on complementary strengths and high trust
- Truth four: feedback is vital but it is not an event; coaching in the moment is what improves performance
- Truth five: ratings and labels detract from performance; personalised growth actions build it
- Truth six: wellbeing drives performance, individually and collectively
