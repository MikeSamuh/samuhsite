## Summary

A conversation with Tara Thiagarajan, founder of Sapien Labs, in partnership with Sapien Labs. Tara defines the social self as how we see ourselves with respect to others and how we relate to them: self-worth, self-image, forming and keeping relationships, and cooperating towards a goal. She says the decline in this dimension is large, alarming, and faster than the decline in mood and outlook. One core factor in the Global Mind data is the age of first smartphone: the younger a child gets one, the worse the social self as a young adult. Her explanation is displacement. Hours a day spent on devices are hours not spent interacting with people, and human bonding evolved on cues like facial expression, body language, tone and touch that the virtual world removes. Her closing message is positive: ask how to get our ten thousand hours of social interaction.

## Key points

- The social self covers self-worth, self-image, relationships and cooperation
- It is declining faster than mood and outlook, and worse for younger generations
- Earlier smartphone acquisition is linked to a worse social self in young adulthood
- Tara cites five to nine hours a day on devices as time displaced from human interaction
- Bonding relies on in-person cues that are eliminated online, so social skill is not learned
- The social self is what lets humans cooperate, which is how the species has progressed
