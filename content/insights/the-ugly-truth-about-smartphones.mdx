## Summary

Rahul walks through a Sapien Labs study in which young adults completed the MHQ, the Mental Health Quotient, and reported the age at which they got their first smartphone. He picks out four findings: mental wellbeing improved with every older age of first ownership, females were more affected than males, the social self was the dimension most impacted, and suicidal thoughts and feelings of aggression also improved with later ownership. He notes that first ownership came earlier in North America, Europe and Australia than in Africa or South Asia. His conclusion is that we should treat smartphones for children the way most countries treat driving, with a minimum age, instruction, supervision and a test, rather than handing devices to young children with no structure.

## Key points

- He cites a Sapien Labs study of 28,000 young adults aged 18 to 24 who took the MHQ and reported the age of their first smartphone
- Mental wellbeing improved with every subsequently older age of first smartphone ownership
- Females were consistently more affected than males by early ownership
- The social self, how we see ourselves in relation to others, was the dimension most impacted
- He compares the issue to driving: age limits, instruction, supervised hours and a test before going solo
- He frames the research as a call to action for parents, educators and policymakers
