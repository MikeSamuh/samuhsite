The year was 1991.

As India embraced economic liberalization, I was in my final year of college, surrounded by soon-to-be graduate peers working feverishly on career choices. Finance and marketing were suddenly touted as fields buzzing with opportunity. The race was on for top MBA spots, with everyone eyeing the hottest new careers.

I was unclear, but fascinated by business management. I found myself in the expansive JNU library, searching for answers. I stumbled upon a book that introduced the fundamentals of business: accounting, finance, operations, sales. None of it sparked my interest. Then, I turned the page to personnel management.

It was not the most glamorous description: hiring, firing, negotiating with unions. But to me, it felt like the heartbeat of an organization, the key to expanding human potential. It was love at first sight.

It took another year and countless rejections before I finally secured a spot in an HR MBA program. Thank you, SIBM. "Love is not always easy, but it is worth it."

Thirty years into my career, I am still in awe of its potential. HR is not just about processes. It is about empowering people to be, and perform, at their best.

Fellow HR professionals, what do you believe is the North Star for our profession?
