## Summary

A conversation with Tara Thiagarajan, founder of Sapien Labs and the Global Mind Project, in partnership with Sapien Labs. Tara explains that the goal is to understand how a rapidly changing environment affects the human mind, and what that means for individuals and society. She describes the finding she did not expect: mental wellbeing has been declining from generation to generation everywhere, not only in the West, with almost half of 18 to 24 year olds struggling. She contrasts that with studies before 2010 which showed young adults with better psychological wellbeing than older groups. Her hope is that large, real-time data can identify the environmental drivers, and that a measurement tool can be used in schools, companies and at a national level to track change and target prevention.

## Key points

- The Global Mind Project began collecting data in 2019
- Mental wellbeing has declined generation to generation across the world, not just the West
- Tara cites almost half of 18 to 24 year olds struggling, and about a third with suicidal thoughts
- Before 2010, studies showed young adults doing better than older age groups
- Large-scale, real-time data is meant to reveal what in the environment is driving the change
- Measuring wellbeing allows organisations and countries to manage it
