## Summary

Rahul takes on the objection he hears most often when he argues for dropping performance ratings: how do we pay people without them? Companies make two or three pay decisions, base pay, bonus and in some cases equity, and ratings conflate all of them. He separates them. Base pay belongs to the job role and its position in the market, so it should track market worth, not ratings. Bonus is where performance belongs, and drawing on Josh Bersin's power-curve research he argues hyper-contributors should receive proportionately more, most people proportionately less, and those who missed expectations none. Equity should follow the logic of buying stock: give it to people who show promise of significant future contribution, which may or may not be the same people performing best today. None of these decisions requires a rating. To guard against bias, he suggests leaders decide together so they can challenge and support each other.

## Key points

- Base pay is about the market value of the role, not individual performance
- Bonus is where performance should show, in proportion to contribution
- Equity should be granted on expected future contribution, like buying shares in a company
- None of the three decisions needs a performance rating
- Bias is handled by leaders making pay decisions together rather than alone
- The result, he argues, is fairer and more equitable than a rating system
