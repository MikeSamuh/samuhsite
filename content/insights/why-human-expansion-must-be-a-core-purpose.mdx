## Premise

I believe that human expansion must be a core purpose for organizations. Money must no longer remain the sole measure for human progress or serve as a proxy for individual, societal or planetary wellbeing.

What if, through the course of one's student life, or as a professional, we gain in physical health, be intellectually energized, feel emotionally fulfilled, and remain grounded and nourished in our raison d'être?

What if we redefine success to truly reflect the interconnectedness of all existence?

What if organizations, educational, governmental, and corporate, were measured on four dimensions, and not just one? Did they fulfill their financial goals? Did their actions lead to a more inclusive and just society? Did they leave the planet better than they found it? Did they enable the wellbeing of their people? With each dimension having equal weighting.

What if education expands to realms beyond knowledge and skills? Like learning how to access the innate intelligence resident in our body, the unique gifts we are here to offer to the world, the inextricable connectedness between us and all of the universe, and the timeless wisdom that what is good for oneself must be good for others.

What if we architect performance differently from a paradigm that is overly centered on individual outcomes (grades, performance ratings) to one that fosters both personal excellence and collective wellbeing? One which does not diminish us into labels or categories.

What can we learn about ourselves and the world if we draw inspiration from the mystical realm and fuse it with modern science?

Through this booklet, I want to question prevalent norms propelled by a Wall Street culture that considers profit as the ultimate purpose of an organization. I seek to inspire ways of working and living that allow us to be in harmony with ourselves, our fellow beings, and that are aligned to existential principles that we, as a collective humanity, have learnt through the ages.

## Issues in contemporary society

In an article published in the Atlantic in October 2017, Eli Cook beautifully captured a phenomenon that has been taking root through the course of the past two centuries. He traced how money became the measure for everything. Largely propelled by the pre-eminent world power, the United States, gradually it was embraced around the globe. For the first time in human history, there has been a concerted effort to measure all forms of progress, especially human wellbeing, purely in economic terms. He concludes with the lines "Nevertheless, by making capital accumulation synonymous with progress, money-based metrics have turned human betterment into a secondary concern. By the early 21st century, American society's top priority became its bottom line, net worth became synonymous with self-worth, and a billionaire businessman who repeatedly pointed to his own wealth as proof of his fitness for office was elected president."

In more recent decades, another phenomenon has taken hold over society: the digital revolution. The internet connected (much of) the globe. Then the smartphone arrived and the connected world landed into the palms of our hands. In just over a decade since its launch, people are incapable of carrying out their daily lives without their mobile phones, accessing them nearly a hundred times in a day. Our closest and most cherished relationship by far, based on time spent and the fierceness with which we protect it, is with an object.

This digital explosion has become rocket fuel for a world that was already in an unrelenting pursuit of growth. Both corporations and countries aim to outcompete each other in their quest for a mythical techno-commercial nirvana. This frenzy has left a long shadow in its wake, three seemingly irreversible global trends, led by the developed world:

1. Steadily deteriorating human wellbeing, with pronounced impact on younger generations
2. Ever widening economic disparities with far fewer beneficiaries
3. A profound disaggregation between our sense of self, the society around us, and the planet we reside in

### Psychological (and overall) wellbeing is declining

Dr. Tara Thiagarajan has dedicated her career to studying the evolution of the human brain. She has a unique vantage point. When her father, the founder of a micro-finance organization in India, fell critically ill, she was deep into her postdoctoral research in a neuroscience lab in Stanford University. Responsibility for this Chennai based organization suddenly fell on her shoulders and for the next 15 years, Tara (with three young children in tow) criss-crossed the oceans to sustain and grow the mission oriented organization. She began to study the dichotomies of the developed and underdeveloped worlds from the perspective of the human brain. Years later, she set up Sapien Labs to further the study of evolution of the brain. Tara and the team of scientists at Sapien Labs created the most comprehensive and validated assessment of mental health globally, called MHQ (mental health quotient). Within three years of its launch in 2019, a million people around the world have taken the assessment, generating the most extensive data set on the state of the mind. They have unearthed startling trends that we must take note of and address urgently.

Here are the key findings included in their March 2022 report:

1. Alarming plummet in mental wellbeing in progressively younger generations in every country sampled
2. Mental wellbeing was lowest overall in English-speaking countries and highest in Latin American and African countries, though the decline is widespread
3. Worse mental-health impact in the internet-dominated (developed) world
4. MHQ scores of countries significantly negatively correlated with cultural indicators such as Performance Orientation and Individualism as well as economic indicators such as GDP and GNI per capita

The scientists pose a profound question. "Thus we are left to ponder the following: that perhaps a system that relentlessly sorts us into performers and non-performers in the singular service of economic growth is not the path to human wellbeing."

### Economic disparities are rapidly widening

Ray Dalio, the multi-billionaire founder of the world's largest hedge fund, Bridgewater Associates, began to notice new patterns in the geo-political-economic landscape after over five decades of his investing career. He decided to zoom back into 500 years of human history to make sense of the present. The widening polarities across the political and economic realms were not new, and pointed to a time of great inflection in a changing world order.

While economic disparities have been on the rise for well over a century, just in the past decade we have witnessed a rise in inequality, unprecedented in living memory. Handfuls of people have amassed disproportionate wealth at the expense of the vast majority. According to a January 2023 Oxfam report, titled "Survival of the Richest", in the last 10 years:

- Billionaires have doubled their wealth, making nearly six times more than the increase in wealth seen by the bottom 50 percent
- For every $100 of wealth created in the last 10 years, $54.40 went to the top 1 percent and $0.70 went to the bottom 50 percent
- The top 1 percent have gained 74 times more wealth than the bottom 50 percent in the last 10 years
- In 2009, 380 billionaires had as much wealth as the bottom 50 percent of the world. By end 2022, a mere 81 had amassed that amount

The pandemic turbo charged these disparities further. "For every dollar the bottom 90 percent have gained, billionaires have gained $1.7m."

Disparities are not restricted to the billionaire class alone. Simply put, the rich are getting richer while everyone else becomes poorer. During the worst of the COVID crisis, in addition to the massive toll on human lives around the world, income losses among the poorest 40 percent of humanity were twice as large as among the richest 20 percent.

It is hard to imagine how these widening disparities can sustain a peaceful society. History is flush with instances of violent upheavals and revolutions whenever the select few have garnered riches in vastly disproportionate quantities to the rest of society.

### Rising sense of separation of self

Owen Eastwood is one of the most sought after performance coaches in the world. He works with elite sports teams, business leaders and international organizations. A New Zealander with Maori, Irish and English roots, he has been living in the UK for the past few decades. Owen has spent much of his life seeking enduring truths about tribes and groups that have survived the ages. He applies these principles to help teams perform at their highest levels. The core of his approach, captured beautifully in his unique book, Belonging, is Whakapapa, a deeply spiritual Maori belief that each of us is part of an unbroken chain of people who share sacred identity and culture. It says that the sun shines on us for a period of time before passing on, and it is in that brief window that we leave our legacy for future generations. Owen calls this The Story of Us. Such beliefs are espoused in every culture. One of the most significant verses from ancient India is Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, the world is one family.

Contemporary western society, however, bears little resemblance to this wisdom from the ages. Children grow up in nuclear families living separately from their grandparents and cousins. From early on they are performance graded in schools. Crossing the threshold of teenage years into adulthood is synonymous with leaving their parents' home. For many, entry into work life is under the shadow of crushing student debt. The corporate world then sorts them into high, average or low performers, with material consequences to each outcome. When an economic down cycle hits, all bets are off as the market punishes companies for "inefficiency" and rewards them for "clearing the deadwood", further strengthening the belief that each is to their own. As life progresses, a prolonged illness might bankrupt the less fortunate. For those with means, old age would be living out one's final years in a care facility isolated from their loved ones.

Admittedly, this is an overly bleak and somewhat Americanized image of a seemingly opulent, progressive and civilized western world. Yet the fundamental tenets underpinning such a society must be questioned when "success" becomes the preserve of a select few, relentless consumption ravages the planet, extreme polarization permeates throughout the society, and psychological and human wellbeing plummets to lowest levels in recorded history.

## Why human expansion must become a core purpose

"We can't solve problems by using the same kind of thinking we used when we created them." Albert Einstein.

In my recent explorations, I have come in contact with wise beings who have dedicated themselves to the service of others. What I have learnt from them takes Einstein's famous words up a level. We cannot access the same consciousness that we used to get to our current state to evolve to the next. Remaining in our current consciousness is inviting history to play in an endless loop. Evolution is about transcending to a higher state.

In a simplistic sense, I see the last several centuries as a cycle where we, the human species, increasingly tapped into our capacity to think and to act. Brain and brawn. We created societal and national norms that valued economic and geographic expansion, and therefore educational systems that celebrated cleverness and to a lesser extent physical strength. We learnt that the path to success was paved by outcompeting, overpowering, conquering or destroying others.

This has become a zero sum game. Transcending this state requires that we augment our formidable intellectual and physical capacities with others, such as an emotional capability (intelligence of the heart) or an instinctual capability (gut wisdom). Or, tap into the biggest one of all, that of surrendering to a higher realm and seeking guidance to fulfill the reason we find ourselves here on this planet.

Human expansion is about being attuned to the intelligence of our entire being, body, mind, heart and soul, and the interconnectedness between ourselves and everything around us. If educational institutions commit themselves to this goal, they can expand their curriculum to newer realms for children to gain access to crucial, self-sustaining life skills such as breath work, body work and accessing the mystical for guidance.

Organizations who do so will change who and how they recruit, their training, performance orientation and incentive structures. They will fundamentally reimagine their purpose, and alter relationships with competitors and employees, and expand their stakeholders to many groups in the society.

We may finally begin walking a path that is sustainable for all.

Human Expansion = physically healthy + mentally energized + emotionally fulfilled + spiritually aligned
