## Summary

Rahul starts from Eli Cook's argument, in a 2017 Atlantic article, that from the mid 19th century everything in American society came to be measured in economic terms and that this spread around the world. He argues the digital revolution and the smartphone gave that trend rocket fuel, and that countries, companies and institutions have been out-competing each other in pursuit of a techno-commercial nirvana. The result, in his reading, is three interrelated trends: deteriorating human wellbeing that hits each younger generation harder, accelerating economic disparity, and a disaggregation of our sense of self and our relationships with others, society and the planet. He proposes two shifts. First, redefine success as four-fold: financially viable, more inclusive, more sustainable, and committed to human wellbeing for all an organisation serves. Second, make wellbeing a core purpose of organisations, so that through work or education people gain in physical health, mental energy, emotional fulfilment and spiritual grounding.

## Key points

- He draws on Eli Cook's account of how money became the measure of all progress
- The digital revolution and the smartphone accelerated the pursuit of money as the only measure
- Three trends follow: declining wellbeing, growing disparity, and a weakened sense of self and connection
- Success should be four-fold: financially viable, inclusive, sustainable, and committed to wellbeing
- Wellbeing should be a core purpose of organisations, not a side benefit
- He is calling for people across every field to join forces
