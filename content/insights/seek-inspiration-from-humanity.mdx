## From Rahul

As posted on samuh.work with this episode.

It might or might not have been Albert Einstein who said “Learn from yesterday, live for today, hope for tomorrow.”

It is worth a ponder.

Homo sapiens first reportedly appeared on earth 200,000 years ago. Considering a global average human lifespan of 73.4 years (WHO, 2019), that was a stunning 2,755 life times ago! All through the ages and in every great civilization, our ancestors, thinkers, philosophers, sages, took pains to capture their learnings in scriptures and texts for the benefit those who were to follow. Much of their teachings were about living a positive, healthy life in harmony with our fellow beings. Even today, there are inspiring examples of people living lives in resonance with such principles.

Tune into this 3 minute video on what we can learn from some of the happiest and longest living people on the planet.

## Summary

Rahul describes a polarising world marked by conflict, a planet in distress and a mental wellbeing crisis, and notes how readily we look for answers in technology: the next app, the next platform, more likes. He argues the answers may be better found in the wisdom humanity has accumulated through earlier calamities and wars, and in people who still live in step with natural rhythms. He cites Héctor García and Francesc Miralles, who studied centenarians in Ogimi, Okinawa, and found the secrets to a long and happy life in staying active, eating mindfully, persevering, low-intensity exercise, reducing stress, sleeping, and nurturing friendships throughout life. They call it ikigai, the reason to wake up each morning.

## Key points

- We tend to seek solutions in the next shiny object rather than in accumulated wisdom
- This is not the first time the world has been in crisis, and the human spirit has recovered before
- The Ogimi centenarians point to habits: activity, mindful eating, perseverance, rest, and friendship
- Ikigai, the reason to wake up each morning, is offered as the thing to find
