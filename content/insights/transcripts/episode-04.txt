Today I want to talk about well-being, specifically mental well-being.
When we talk about mental health, it conjures up images about, well, this is about depression
or anxiety, but really it is more than that. We perceive reality through our mind. And so when
we are mentally not okay, it affects every dimension of our existence from our physical health
to our capacities, to our ability to function in the world. Sadly, research is telling us that there
are three significant concerning trends on mental well-being. The first is that overall mental
well-being, which has been declining for well over a decade now and plummeted during the pandemic,
has really not shown signs of recovery. More significantly, young adults are three to five
times worse off than older generations. The second trend is that of the impact on the social self.
The social self is how we see ourselves in relation to others. Our ability to form positive relationships
to be able to function. And that has experienced the highest drop between the older generations
and the younger generations. And the third trend is the erosion of family relationships and close
friendships. Again, unfortunately, younger adults are half as likely to be close to their families
and three times more likely to not get along with family members at all compared to their grandparents'
generation. Why this matters is because the risk of mental health is 10 times higher for those
that are not close to their families or don't have close friendships compared to those that do.
Unfortunately, this is a widespread global phenomenon. It's affected North America and Europe and
those that we see as more individualistic cultures a bit more perhaps than like Latin
American cultures that are more collectivist. But the decline is widespread and it's global.
So what got us here? While it's hard to be able to pinpoint to every specific cause,
I can probably point to three major ones. The first is an increase in individualism that's been taking hold
for perhaps five or six decades at this point in the more affluent developed world, but then spreading
more broadly. And this move has been propagated by migration to larger cities, for instance.
But also, it is this kind of more individualistic culture that has contributed to an impact on the
social self. Second, money as the measure of all progress. And that results in many of us that work
experiencing higher and higher levels of stress, the stress to be able to deliver a quarter, to be able to
exceed expectations of investors. And that stress unfortunately seeps into every dimension of our lives and affects it.
And third, the digital revolution. The smartphone, which ostensibly connected us to people all around the world and
gave us the convenience to press a button and get our groceries, on the other hand, has reduced our need to be with
other people in person and has created a more sedentary lifestyle, thus causing greater mental illness.
So the question is, what do we do about these very concerning trends?
