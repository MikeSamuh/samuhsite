Today I want to talk about habits. Making and sustaining good habits. Now much has been written
on habits and a lot of it is actually good and true. For example James Clear in his book Atomic
Habits talks about a four-step framework. Cue, craving, response and reward. And that's how
I made my habit of flossing daily. After procrastinating for many years I finally
decided to keep the floss right next to my toothbrush. That I couldn't miss it. So that
was the cue. And then every time I brushed my teeth it was right there making me want to reach out to
it. The craving. And then I would actually floss. That's the response. And as soon as I was done
I'd feel gratified. Meaning the reward. But if it was that simple to form new habits why isn't it
that we are forming great habits all the time? Why is it that new year resolutions start off with a bang
and end with a whimper? More on that to come. And first please click like and subscribe.
The reason that habits don't stick is because we are missing one crucial factor in the habits loop.
And that is our environment. Our environment. Meaning our team, our home, our organization, our community
has a profound influence in our ability to sustain good habits. Renowned team performance coach
Owen Eastwood who has worked with some of the world's elite sports and corporate teams
says that 70% of human behavior is driven by the environment they are in. And so if our immediate
environment is not supportive of our new habits they are highly unlikely to stick. So how could we make
good habits stick? Well for that let's take a cue from wisdom cultures. And the way they do that
is by ritualizing them. Why rituals you might ask? Because rituals unlike individual habits are done
collectively. And recent research has shown that when rituals are physical as in they have an action or word
psychological as in they have a meaning or significance to them and communal or done collectively
together they can actually improve group performance. Let's take eating for example. It has been shown that we
eat less when we eat together than we would when we eat alone. So if you want to sleep better, eat healthier,
exercise more often, don't make a new year resolution. Instead, go make a ritual with your team, be it at home or at work.
Because when you do that you will keep each other accountable and support one another when something falls off.
And more importantly, you will actually enhance your performance. Go ahead, try a ritual.
